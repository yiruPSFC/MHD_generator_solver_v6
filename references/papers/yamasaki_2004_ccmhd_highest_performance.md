Achievement of the Highest Performance of a
CCMHD Generator: An Isentropic Efﬁciency of 63%
and an Enthalpy Extraction Ratio of 31%
Tomoyuki Murakami, Member, IEEE, Yoshihiro Okuno, and Hiroyuki Yamasaki
Abstract—In this paper we describe the highest-performance
closed-cycle magnetohydrodynamic (CCMHD) power generator
driven by a shock-tube. Despite the use of a small-scale device
having a power-generating volume of approximately 4 liters,
outstanding performance, namely, the isentropic efﬁciency of
63%, the enthalpy extraction ratio of 30.8%, the electrical power
output of 1.23 MW, and the power density of 297 MW/m
/51,i s
achieved at the stagnation temperature of 2250 K and the applied
magnetic ﬂux density of 3.0 T. The following facts contribute to the
world’s highest performance of the CCMHD power generator: a
less-divergent generator shape; the employment of cesium-seeded
helium gas; the introduction of an inlet swirl; and the production
of homogeneous plasma.
Index Terms—Cesium-seeded helium, disk CCMHD generator,
inlet swirl, less-divergent channel, plasma structure.
I. I NTRODUCTION
A
CLOSED-CYCLE magnetohydrodynamic (CCMHD)
power generator using a nonequilibrium plasma has
been drawing attention as a novel energy conversion device
[1]. Since a high-temperature gas of approximately 2000 K is
used, the CCMHD generator offers better energy conversion
efﬁciency than conventional devices. Different from turbine de-
vices, the CCMHD generator directly converts the enthalpy of
a high-temperature gas to electrical energy without any rotating
parts. Because of its small inertial force, the CCMHD generator
can control the power output in milliseconds. This “rapid” op-
erator performance is advantageous as a pulsed-power source,
as well as a continuous-working facility connected to electrical
power systems [2].
A number of CCMHD power-generation experiments using
shock-tube pulsed-power facilities have been conducted in
different institutes and nations, where various ﬂow-mode types,
channel shapes, and working media were examined. The ad-
vantages of a swirl-ﬂow mode [3] over a radial-ﬂow mode in
disk-shaped generators have been shown. Shock-tube experi-
ments at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) [4]
and Eindhoven University of Technology (EUT) [5] showed
that better enthalpy extraction ratio could be achieved using
swirl-ﬂow generators rather than radial-ﬂow generators. In
those studies, high-temperature cesium-seeded argon (Ar-Cs)
gas was used as a working medium in order to obtain a high

electrical conductivity, a high power output, and a high en-
thalpy extraction ratio. At the Tokyo Institute of Technology
(Tokyo Tech), a very high enthalpy extraction ratio of 31.6%
was achieved using a swirl-ﬂow generator with cesium-seeded
helium (He-Cs), where helium gas provides a higher ﬂow
velocity than argon gas at the same Mach number [6]. These
swirl-ﬂow generators of MIT, EUT, and Tokyo Tech have rather
large divergent channel shapes, which are advantageous for
achieving a higher enthalpy extraction ratio [7].
On the other hand, recent theoretical analysis revealed that
future commercial generators will require a high isentropic ef-
ﬁciency as well as a high enthalpy extraction ratio [8], so that
the generator shape has to be less divergent. Thus, shock-tube
experiments using a less-divergent swirl-ﬂow generator with
Ar-Cs were conducted [9]. Due to an inherent small total pres-
sure loss, the less-divergent swirl-ﬂow generator obtained a high
isentropic efﬁciency of 54% and an enthalpy extraction ratio of
25.7%.
The present experimental study aims to achieve the highest
isentropic efﬁciency and enthalpy extraction ratio of a shock-
tube-driven disk CCMHD generator. For this purpose, in the
experimental setup 1) a less-divergent generator is employed;
2) cesium-seeded helium is selected as a working medium, and
3) an inlet swirl is introduced.
In order to describe electrical characteristics, plasma-ﬂuid
conditions, and generator performance, the following inves-
tigation methods are used: one-dimensional electrical and
ﬂuid-dynamical measurements, two-dimensional plasma vi-
sualization, and local plasma-ﬂuid-dynamical calculations.
Furthermore, the present generator performance is compared
with previous experimental results.

The measured electron temperature is 4300 K; which sug-
gests that the plasma is in a fully ionized seed condition. An
electrical conductivity of approximately 210 S/m and a Hall pa-
rameter of approximately 5 are estimated in the plasma. The
He-Cs plasma has an inherent feature that the electron number
density is nearly constant at the electron temperature of 4000 K
to 10 000 K. At this range of temperature, the cesium atoms are
completely ionized but ionization of the helium atoms is negli-
gible. The plasma in the fully ionized seed condition is known
to be stable against the ionization instability and is spatially uni-
form [17].

IV . CONCLUSION
The CCMHD power generation experiments using a nonequi-
librium supersonic plasma were conducted using a shock-tube
facility. In order to achieve the high performance, the exper-
imental setup was arranged as follows: 1) the less-divergent
geometry of the generator to obtain the high isentropic ef ﬁ-
ciency; 2) the employment of cesium-seeded helium to achieve
a high-velocity ﬂow ﬁeld and a stable and uniform plasma; and
3) the introduction of an inlet swirl using simple thin vanes. In
addition to the above facts, the production of a homogeneous
fully ionized seed plasma contributes to the outstanding perfor-
mance, namely, the isentropic ef ﬁciency of 63%, the enthalpy
extraction ratio of 30.8%, the maximum electrical power output
of 1.23 MW, and the maximum power density of 297 MW/m
at the stagnation temperature of 2250 K, the stagnation pressure
of 0.14 MPa, and the applied magnetic ﬂux density of 3.0 T.
